# Canadian resume conventions for a new-grad tech or data role in Toronto

Researched 2026-11-06. Sources are Canadian career centres, the Government of Canada, the Ontario Human Rights Commission (OHRC), and Google's former head of people operations. Each claim links to the page it came from. "Not verified" marks a claim I could not confirm on a primary page.

## Answer

The case is a new grad with an Ontario college diploma in a computing program, one co-op term, side projects, and several years in a prior non-tech career. The target is a Toronto tech or data role.

1. Aim for one page. Canadian career centres accept one or two pages ([U of T toolkit](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf), [Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/write-good-resume), [Seneca Works](https://students.senecapolytechnic.ca/spaces/138/seneca-works/wiki/view/1878/resum%C3%A9-writing)). The only tech-specific source says one page ([U of T CS recap of a Google workshop](https://www.teach.cs.toronto.edu/ambassadors/blog/2025-events/google_resume_workshop)). The prior-career years do not need full detail for a data role, so they should not push the resume to two pages. A second page is acceptable in Canada if the content earns it.
2. Use this section order: header, a short highlights of qualifications (3 to 4 bullets), education, technical skills, relevant experience (the co-op), projects, then previous experience (the prior career, condensed). Seneca's own order puts highlights, then education, then work or project experience ([Seneca Works](https://students.senecapolytechnic.ca/spaces/138/seneca-works/wiki/view/1878/resum%C3%A9-writing)). Education goes high because the diploma is the credential that explains the career change.
3. Use a bulleted "Highlights of Qualifications" (also called "Summary of Qualifications"), not a prose profile and not an objective. The skills come from different settings (a co-op, projects, and a prior career). UTM names that as the case where a summary helps ([UTM summary handout](https://utm.utoronto.ca/careers/media/6593/download?inline=)).
4. Write each bullet as action verb, then what and how (tools), then result with a number. That is the Canadian career-centre formula, and it is close to Google's XYZ. Use no "I" or "me" ([Seneca Works](https://students.senecapolytechnic.ca/spaces/138/seneca-works/wiki/view/1878/resum%C3%A9-writing), [U of T toolkit](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)). Give the co-op 3 to 5 bullets, each project 2 to 3, and the prior-career role 2 to 3.
5. Header has name, "Toronto, ON", phone, email, LinkedIn and GitHub. Leave out the photo, age, date of birth, marital status, SIN, full street address and any references line.
6. Use Canadian spelling consistently (labour, centre, organize, analyze). Write dates as abbreviated month and year ("Jan 2025 – Aug 2025"). Do not use numeric dates.
7. Education line: "Ontario College Diploma, <Program>" plus "<College>, Toronto, ON" plus "Expected <Mon YYYY>". Drop "Expected" once the diploma is granted.
8. Use a grouped skills section with one line per category: Languages, Data, Tools, Platforms. Order each line from strongest to weakest.

## 1. Length

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| U of T Career Exploration and Education toolkit | "In Canada, most employers expect to see a resumé that is one to two pages long. Be sure to check the standards within your sector and location." ([U of T toolkit, p. 6](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)) |
| Job Bank (Government of Canada) | "Limit your resume to two pages." ([Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/write-good-resume)) |
| Seneca Works | "one to two pages with an effective use of white space" ([Seneca Works](https://students.senecapolytechnic.ca/spaces/138/seneca-works/wiki/view/1878/resum%C3%A9-writing)) |
| York Co-op and Career Centre checklist | "Is your resumé one or two full pages in length?" ([York checklist](https://www.yorku.ca/career-ready/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/10/ResumeChecklist.pdf)) |
| U of T Computer Science recap of a Google resume workshop | "Keep your resume to 1 page." ([U of T CS ambassadors blog](https://www.teach.cs.toronto.edu/ambassadors/blog/2025-events/google_resume_workshop)) |
| TMU Creative Industries | Content should fit "neatly on one page." ([TMU Creative Industries](https://www.torontomu.ca/creativeindustries/internships/creative-industries-internship-and-career-resources/resume-layout-appearance/)) |

The sources disagree in part. General Canadian guidance from U of T, Job Bank, Seneca and York allows two pages. The tech-specific source (U of T CS, relaying Google) and one TMU faculty page say one page. York adds that a two-page resume should fill both pages ("full pages"). Job Bank also says to "Place the emphasis of your resume on your most recent experience" ([Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/write-good-resume)). For a junior tech applicant, one page follows the tech-specific advice and still meets every Canadian source.

Not verified: no Canadian source said whether a prior non-tech career justifies a second page. The Job Bank support page says to hide "your oldest and least relevant jobs" and to "Only show the work experiences and credentials that are most relevant to the position" ([Job Bank support, work experience](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/support/question?qaid=80&tid=57)). That points toward condensing the prior career rather than expanding it.

## 2. Section order

The sources agree that order follows relevance to the job. They give different defaults.

- U of T: "Personal information appears first, but after that, the order of these sections depends upon how you wish to market your experience" ([U of T toolkit, p. 4](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)).
- Seneca Works default order: Header, Highlights of Skills/Qualifications, Education, Work Experience or Relevant Project Work, Volunteer Experience ([Seneca Works](https://students.senecapolytechnic.ca/spaces/138/seneca-works/wiki/view/1878/resum%C3%A9-writing)).
- Waterloo Centre for Career Development, current workshop: Contact Information, Summary of Qualifications, Education, Work Experience, Volunteer Experience, Project Experience, Awards and Certificates, Activities and Interests. It also says "Prioritize sections based on relevance" ([Waterloo, Creating an Effective Résumé](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/creating-an-effective-resume.pdf)).
- Waterloo, graduate-student workshop: Header, Summary of Qualifications, Relevant Experience, Additional Experience, Selected Projects, Education, Technical Skills ([Waterloo, Resumes and Cover Letters](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/resumes-and-cover-letters.pdf)). This puts Education after experience, for people with relevant experience.
- U of T CS recap of the Google workshop lists the must-have sections as contact info with LinkedIn and GitHub, education, technical skills, work experience, projects, and leadership or activities. It lists no summary ([U of T CS ambassadors blog](https://www.teach.cs.toronto.edu/ambassadors/blog/2025-events/google_resume_workshop)).
- York sample computer science resume: Objective, Education and Achievements with a related project, Employment Experience, Technical Skills ([York CS sample](https://www.yorku.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/08/Sample-Resume-Computer-Sci-1_0-1.pdf)). The sample is dated 2017.

Where projects go: U of T and UTM say to label the section "Academic Projects, Independent Projects, Research Projects or Projects" ([UTM, What's in a Resume](https://www.utm.utoronto.ca/careers/resume-cover-letter-resources/whats-resume)). Seneca allows "Relevant Project Work" in place of work experience ([Seneca Works](https://students.senecapolytechnic.ca/spaces/138/seneca-works/wiki/view/1878/resum%C3%A9-writing)). Waterloo calls the section "Selected Projects" ([Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/resumes-and-cover-letters.pdf)). The U of T CS recap says to put projects on GitHub and link GitHub in the header ([U of T CS ambassadors blog](https://www.teach.cs.toronto.edu/ambassadors/blog/2025-events/google_resume_workshop)).

Where education goes: every new-grad default above (Seneca, Waterloo's current deck, York, U of T CS) puts education near the top. Only the Waterloo graduate deck puts it lower.

## 3. Summary, profile, summary of qualifications, or none

York names three kinds of introduction and says to "Choose only ONE" ([York, Targeted Résumé Introductions](https://www.yorku.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/08/Targeted-Resume-Introductions-Accomplishments-1.pdf)):

- Career Objective. One sentence naming the target position and 2 to 3 reasons.
- Highlights of Qualifications, "also called a Profile, Qualifications or Highlights". These are bullets giving "the 5-7 best reasons".
- Profile. Prose, "generally no more than 4-6 lines of text."

The recommended bullet count differs by source:

| Source | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| U of T toolkit | A summary section at the top "is strongly recommended", with "three to five bullet points" ([U of T toolkit, p. 4](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)) |
| Seneca Works | "List four to six bulleted points relevant to the position" ([Seneca Works](https://students.senecapolytechnic.ca/spaces/138/seneca-works/wiki/view/1878/resum%C3%A9-writing)) |
| Waterloo CCD | "Highlight 4 – 6 qualifications", "reference where and/or how you developed the skill", "strongly recommend using keywords from posting" ([Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/resumes-and-cover-letters.pdf)). The section may be titled "Summary of Qualifications, Skills, Professional Summary, or Profile", and employers "scan this section first" ([Waterloo, Creating an Effective Résumé](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/creating-an-effective-resume.pdf)) |
| York | 5 to 7 bullets for Highlights of Qualifications ([York](https://www.yorku.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/08/Targeted-Resume-Introductions-Accomplishments-1.pdf)) |
| UTM | Optional. Use it when skills come "from a variety of experiences". Skip it "If the first 2-3 key things on your resume ... quickly demonstrate your qualifications". Keep it to the "most relevant 3-5 skills" ([UTM summary handout](https://utm.utoronto.ca/careers/media/6593/download?inline=)) |
| U of T CS recap of the Google workshop | No summary in the must-have list ([U of T CS ambassadors blog](https://www.teach.cs.toronto.edu/ambassadors/blog/2025-events/google_resume_workshop)) |

The bulleted "Highlights of Qualifications" or "Summary of Qualifications" is the Canadian career-centre default for students and new grads. UTM and the tech-specific source treat it as optional. Waterloo's ATS tips list "Consider including a summary section" ([Waterloo, Creating an Effective Résumé](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/creating-an-effective-resume.pdf)). Each bullet should name where the skill came from. Waterloo and UTM both say so, and UTM's examples follow the pattern "Developed data management skills by maintaining the patient database ... for over 200 patients" ([UTM summary handout](https://utm.utoronto.ca/careers/media/6593/download?inline=)).

## 4. Bullet style

Canadian career centres teach one formula under different names: action verb, what you did and how, and the result.

- U of T: "Accomplishment statements describe what you did (the action), the context (audience, methodology used, tools applied) and the result or outcome / impact." Format: "Action Verb, what you did, purpose/impact" ([U of T toolkit, p. 6](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)).
- Waterloo: "What + How + Why", or "Achievement Verb (what+why) + How you did it" ([Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/resumes-and-cover-letters.pdf)). Its 2026 deck gives the HOW example "Using Excel VBA, Python" and the WHY example "Increased sales by 15%" ([Waterloo, Creating an Effective Résumé](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/creating-an-effective-resume.pdf)).
- York: "Option 1 Action Verb, What you did / How you did it, Result/Impact" or "Option 2 Action Verb, Result/Impact, What you did / How you did it" ([York checklist](https://www.yorku.ca/career-ready/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/10/ResumeChecklist.pdf)).
- TMU Creative Industries: "action verb + example + outcome" ([TMU](https://www.torontomu.ca/creativeindustries/internships/creative-industries-internship-and-career-resources/resume-layout-appearance/)).
- Job Bank: "persuasive verbs such as handled, managed, led, developed" ([Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/write-good-resume)).

STAR and CAR: I found no Canadian career centre that tells students to write resume bullets in STAR form. York uses STAR for "Answering Behavioural Interview Questions" ([York, describe your experiences](https://www.yorku.ca/career-ready/resources-library/describe-your-experiences-in-a-way-that-resonates-with-employers/)). Not verified: a search snippet says the Ontario Public Service hiring guide recommends PAR (Problem, Action, Result) for interviews ([Ontario, careers hiring process](https://www.ontario.ca/page/careers-hiring-process)). I did not read that page directly.

Google XYZ: Laszlo Bock, then Google's SVP of People Operations, wrote: "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]." His example of a stronger bullet is "Improved portfolio performance by 12% ($1.2M) over one year" ([Bock, LinkedIn, 2014](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/20140929001534-24454816-my-personal-formula-for-a-better-resume)). In an earlier New York Times interview he worded it as "I accomplished X, relative to Y, by doing Z" (quoted by [Quartz](https://qz.com/201088/google-just-revealed-the-incredibly-simple-formula-for-killer-resumes). I could not fetch the NYT original). The U of T CS recap of a 2025 Google workshop repeats it: "Accomplished X (what you did), as measured by Y (the metric/result), by doing Z (the actions you took)" ([U of T CS ambassadors blog](https://www.teach.cs.toronto.edu/ambassadors/blog/2025-events/google_resume_workshop)). I found no current page on google.com that states the formula. The Google "How we hire" page has no resume guidance ([Google, how we hire](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/how-we-hire/)).

XYZ and the Canadian formula contain the same parts in a different order. XYZ leads with the result, which matches York's "Option 2." Either order is consistent with Canadian advice.

Bullets per entry:

- UTM: "three to five accomplishment statements for each job or project" ([UTM](https://www.utm.utoronto.ca/careers/resume-cover-letter-resources/whats-resume)).
- Job Bank: "limiting each resume section or sub section to 5-7 bullet points" ([Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/write-good-resume)).

Length per bullet: not verified. No primary source I read gives a line or word limit per bullet. The career-centre examples run one to two lines each ([York](https://www.yorku.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/08/Targeted-Resume-Introductions-Accomplishments-1.pdf), [Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/creating-an-effective-resume.pdf)).

First person:

- Seneca: "avoid first person language (i.e., 'I' and 'me')" ([Seneca Works](https://students.senecapolytechnic.ca/spaces/138/seneca-works/wiki/view/1878/resum%C3%A9-writing)).
- U of T: "Use an active voice to describe experiences and avoid using personal pronouns", with past tense for past roles and present tense for current ones ([U of T toolkit, p. 4](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)).
- Exception: one UTM worked example uses "where I led five students" ([UTM summary handout](https://utm.utoronto.ca/careers/media/6593/download?inline=)). The two main guides above outweigh it.
- U of T also says not to start bullets with "responsibilities included" or "duties" ([U of T toolkit, p. 6](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)).

## 5. What to omit

| Item | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Photo | Job Bank: a photo "is not the norm in Canada" ([Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/write-good-resume)). OHRC: "employers should not request photos of potential employees" ([OHRC, designing application forms](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/iv-human-rights-issues-all-stages-employment/4-designing-application-forms)) |
| Age, date of birth | OHRC: "No questions about age are permitted on an application form, other than whether an applicant is 18 years or over" and "Employers should not ask for the date of birth" ([OHRC](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/iv-human-rights-issues-all-stages-employment/4-designing-application-forms)). Job Bank: leave out "age, weight, height, marital status" ([Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/write-good-resume)) |
| Marital status | OHRC: "No questions about marital status are permitted" ([OHRC](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/iv-human-rights-issues-all-stages-employment/4-designing-application-forms)) |
| Citizenship, health | U of T: "height, health, marital status, citizenship and personal photos are not included" ([U of T toolkit, p. 4](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)). OHRC allows only "Are you legally entitled to work in Canada?" ([OHRC](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/iv-human-rights-issues-all-stages-employment/4-designing-application-forms)). The application form may ask that question. The resume does not need to answer it |
| SIN | Job Bank: "never include your Social Insurance Number in your resume" ([Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/write-good-resume)). OHRC: a SIN "may be requested after a candidate has received a conditional offer" ([OHRC](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/iv-human-rights-issues-all-stages-employment/4-designing-application-forms)) |
| Driver's licence | OHRC: request only "after making a conditional offer" ([OHRC](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/iv-human-rights-issues-all-stages-employment/4-designing-application-forms)) |
| References line | Job Bank: "Keep references on a separate sheet and provide them only when they are specifically requested" ([Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/write-good-resume)). U of T: "do not include or refer to references in your resumé" ([U of T toolkit, p. 4](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)). York: "References section not needed" ([York checklist](https://www.yorku.ca/career-ready/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/10/ResumeChecklist.pdf)). All three agree, so leave out "References available upon request" |
| Full address | Sources disagree. Job Bank says to list "name, address, email and phone number" ([Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/write-good-resume)), and Seneca says to include "address" ([Seneca Works](https://students.senecapolytechnic.ca/spaces/138/seneca-works/wiki/view/1878/resum%C3%A9-writing)). Waterloo says "Location: optional and can be kept general (e.g., Waterloo, ON)" ([Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/resumes-and-cover-letters.pdf)). U of T's sample headers use "Toronto, ON" with no street ([U of T toolkit, samples](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)). Seneca notes that "many systems are set up to search and qualify candidates based on location" ([Seneca Works](https://students.senecapolytechnic.ca/spaces/138/seneca-works/wiki/view/1878/resum%C3%A9-writing)). City and province meet both concerns |

Phone: UTM says use a number "you check regularly and should have voicemail enabled", and keep contact information to "two and four lines" ([UTM](https://www.utm.utoronto.ca/careers/resume-cover-letter-resources/whats-resume)). The U of T and York samples write numbers as 416-987-6543 and 905-800-5690, with the area code and hyphens and no +1 ([U of T toolkit](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf), [York CS sample](https://www.yorku.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/08/Sample-Resume-Computer-Sci-1_0-1.pdf)). Not verified: no source states a rule on the +1 prefix. U of T also says a personal LinkedIn URL and GitHub belong in the header ([U of T toolkit, p. 4](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)). The U of T CS recap says to use a personal email, not a school one ([U of T CS ambassadors blog](https://www.teach.cs.toronto.edu/ambassadors/blog/2025-events/google_resume_workshop)).

Related: the OHRC says employers "should consider all of your work experience, in any country" and that asking for Canadian experience "can result in discrimination" ([OHRC, removing the Canadian experience barrier](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/removing-canadian-experience-barrier-brochure)). Work done outside Canada belongs on the resume on equal terms.

## 6. Canadian spelling and date formats

Spelling. I could not fetch the Government of Canada Language Portal pages because a bot check blocked them. The points below come from its search-result snippets and are marked accordingly.

- Not verified on page: the Language Portal says the -our spelling (labour) is preferred in Canada, though labor is accepted ([Language Portal, labour/labor](https://our-languages.canada.ca/en/writing-tips-plus/labour-labor-laborious)).
- Not verified on page: the portal uses the Canadian Oxford Dictionary as its spelling authority. That dictionary lists -ize first (organize, analyze), with -ise as a variant ([Language Portal, why Canadian spelling is different](https://our-languages.canada.ca/en/blogue-blog/canadian-spelling-eng), [spelling: international variations](https://our-languages.canada.ca/en/writing-tips-plus/spelling-international-variations)).
- Seen in primary career-centre text: "centre", "colour", "behaviors"/"behaviours" mixed, "organization", "analyzed" ([U of T toolkit](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf), [York](https://www.yorku.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/08/Targeted-Resume-Introductions-Accomplishments-1.pdf)). Canadian career centres themselves are not perfectly consistent.
- Not verified: "program" (not "programme") for software and for academic programs; "licence" as noun and "license" as verb; doubled l in "travelled". These follow Canadian Oxford convention as I understand it, but I did not confirm them on a fetched page.
- Inference: keep product and code names as their owners spell them (for example a "color" parameter). Pick one spelling system and apply it everywhere.

The word itself appears as "resume", "resumé" and "résumé" across Canadian sources (Job Bank, U of T, Waterloo). All three are in use.

Dates. The Canada.ca Content Style Guide, section 4.7 ([Canada.ca style guide](https://design.canada.ca/style-guide/)):

- Write "July 31, 2016", with no ordinal suffix.
- "Abbreviate month names when space is limited (for example, Jan, Feb, Mar)."
- "Use the numeric date format only when space is limited", and then "Write yyyy-mm-dd".

Career-centre samples use month and year: "Jan – Aug 2026" ([Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/creating-an-effective-resume.pdf)) and "Jun 2024 – Apr 2026" ([York checklist](https://www.yorku.ca/career-ready/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/10/ResumeChecklist.pdf)). U of T says to keep "all the dates in the same place" ([U of T toolkit, p. 4](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)). Inference: avoid numeric forms like 03/04/2026. Readers may take them as either day-first or month-first.

## 7. Career change, co-op term, and college diploma

Previous career:

- U of T describes a "Modified chronological format" that groups career-related experience together "while maintaining reverse chronological order". U of T says it is useful "when work experience is scattered or not current" and calls chronological format "the most widely used" and the one "Many employers prefer" ([U of T toolkit, p. 5](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)).
- The same page says the functional format "works best where an individual ... is making a career change" ([U of T toolkit, p. 5](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)). The Seneca page agrees for gaps or little experience ([Seneca Works](https://students.senecapolytechnic.ca/spaces/138/seneca-works/wiki/view/1878/resum%C3%A9-writing)). This conflicts with the employer preference for chronological format. A functional format also hides dates. U of T and Waterloo warn that tables and unusual layouts can trip applicant tracking systems ([U of T toolkit, p. 6](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf), [Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/creating-an-effective-resume.pdf)). For a candidate with a recent co-op and a clear timeline, modified chronological fits better.
- Waterloo and York both suggest splitting experience into "Relevant Experience" and "Additional Experience" ([Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/resumes-and-cover-letters.pdf), [York checklist](https://www.yorku.ca/career-ready/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/10/ResumeChecklist.pdf)). Waterloo says to "Extract transferable skills to use for your bullet points." The co-op goes under Relevant Experience. The prior career goes under Additional or Previous Experience, with 2 to 3 bullets on skills a data employer values: leadership, safety-critical procedure, record keeping, and any data or reporting work.
- Job Bank says to use "industry standard job titles" and to hide the least relevant roles ([Job Bank support](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/support/question?qaid=80&tid=57)). Use the standard industry title for the prior role, not internal shorthand.
- Not verified: no fetched Canadian primary source covers a change from this specific prior field into tech. TMU runs a career-changer workshop on formatting a resume for a career change and addressing gaps, but the page lists topics only ([TMU Chang career changer series](https://www.torontomu.ca/wellbeing-central/2026/03/changcareerchangersseries/)).

Co-op term:

- Waterloo's employer guidance tells employers to avoid the job title "co-op student" and to use "Assistant, analyst, associate", because this "translates better on their resumes for the future" ([Waterloo, job title guidelines](https://uwaterloo.ca/hire/waterlooworks-employer-help/job-title-guidelines)). The job-title line should carry the real role (for example "Systems Developer").
- Not verified: no fetched primary source says whether to add "(Co-op)" after the title. Common Canadian practice marks it somewhere so the reader knows it was a work term. York's checklist does the same for volunteer roles: "Fundraiser, Heart and Stroke Foundation (Volunteer)" ([York checklist](https://www.yorku.ca/career-ready/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/10/ResumeChecklist.pdf)). ## 8. Skills section format

- Waterloo's 2026 example groups skills by category, one line each: "Programming Languages: ...", "Frameworks and Libraries: ...", "Developer Tools: ...", "Technologies: ..." ([Waterloo, Creating an Effective Résumé](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/creating-an-effective-resume.pdf)).
- The York CS sample uses the same grouped approach, with categories for Operating Systems, Programming Languages, Hardware and Software ([York CS sample](https://www.yorku.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/08/Sample-Resume-Computer-Sci-1_0-1.pdf)).
- The U of T CS recap says to order skills "from most to least proficient". It also suggests adding proficiency levels (Proficient, Intermediate, Beginner) ([U of T CS ambassadors blog](https://www.teach.cs.toronto.edu/ambassadors/blog/2025-events/google_resume_workshop)). No other source asks for proficiency labels. Labels cost space, and ordering alone already signals strength.
- York's checklist says to highlight hard skills such as "R, Python, SPSS" ([York checklist](https://www.yorku.ca/career-ready/wp-content/uploads/sites/1080/2026/10/ResumeChecklist.pdf)). Waterloo says skills listed "without supporting evidence" are a top reason resumes get overlooked ([Waterloo, Creating an Effective Résumé](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/creating-an-effective-resume.pdf)). Every skill on the list should appear in at least one bullet.
- For applicant tracking systems, Waterloo says to avoid multiple columns, tables, icons, headers and footers, to "Create in Word, upload as PDF", and to use standard fonts ([Waterloo, Creating an Effective Résumé](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/creating-an-effective-resume.pdf)). Seneca says "Never put information in the header" of the document ([Seneca Works](https://students.senecapolytechnic.ca/spaces/138/seneca-works/wiki/view/1878/resum%C3%A9-writing)). A plain one-column skills block meets both.
